Research Agenda

I study measurement: whether the instruments social science increasingly relies on — large language models used to score political text — actually measure what they claim to, with what reliability, and under what conditions their scores can legitimately be compared across time and settings. My dissertation develops the Measurement, Evaluation and Validation (MEV) framework, which treats constrained LLM evaluators as scientific instruments whose validity, reliability, and comparability must be demonstrated rather than assumed.

Current work extends that discipline to AI evaluation itself, organized around three questions: is the score valid, is it stable, and can the internal signal be trusted. Recent results include certifying that temperature-0 evaluation is bitwise reproducible when its full configuration is pinned — locating evaluation variance in configuration and version change rather than in the models themselves — and automating the dissertation's instrument into a self-validating tool that tracks partisan affect in congressional floor speech over time.

The agenda's aim is a general statistical framework for quantifying evaluator uncertainty across both open-weight and proprietary-model regimes, with reporting standards that let researchers state how much confidence an AI-generated measurement deserves. Substantive applications center on political language over time — partisan affect, ideology, and legislative speech — across comparative and international contexts.

Dissertation

Measuring Political Concepts Over Time with Constrained Large Language Models
Ph.D., Political Science (Political Methodology), Rutgers University — conferred May 17, 2026
Committee: Beth Leech (chair) · Junyi Jessy Li (Linguistics, UT Austin) · Richard Lau (political psychology) · Jan Kubik (interpretive methods)

The dissertation develops the Measurement, Evaluation, and Validation (MEV) framework for measuring latent political concepts — partisan affect, national economic ideology — in text spanning several decades, where the meaning of the concepts themselves drifts. Constrained large language models (fixed output space with human-in-the-loop validation, temperature-0 inference, era-grounded exemplars) serve as the measurement instruments; the constraints support reproducibility and limit annotation, prompt, and automation bias.

Scores are validated against exogenous benchmarks, and a drift taxonomy (semantic, conceptual, predictive) distinguishes change in a concept from change in the instrument. Analyses cover ANES survey text (1984–2020) and extend to congressional floor speech, with fully seeded, reproducible pipelines.

From the dissertation to a research program

The dissertation established that a constrained language model can carry a political construct across four decades of drifting language, validated against a criterion outside the model. That result opened a program rather than closing a question — Measurement Science for AI Evaluation.

The instrument is being pushed further in two directions: automated so that validation runs as part of measurement, and aimed back into survey decades the feeling thermometer never covered. It is also being studied as an object in its own right — whether a validated judge survives being distilled into open-weight models, and whether agreement with a validated teacher is evidence of validity at all. Each study is registered before it is run, read once against a held-out criterion, and adjudicated under rules fixed in advance.

Research Experience

Independent Researcher — AI Evaluation Infrastructure

May 2026 – present · Austin, TX

Measurement on the measurers: validate closed models at the boundary; certify open models from inside the stack; test whether validity survives the copy.

Closed models — is the score valid?

Automating the dissertation's MEV framework into a self-validating text-to-measurement instrument for constrained LLMs (fixed output space, temperature 0, era-grounded exemplars), checking every score against blinded exogenous benchmarks with falsifiable verdicts (validated, refinable, misspecified); deployed beyond its native survey domain onto congressional floor speech to generate an affective measure for Congress over time. Offline tier validated at n = 1200 (r = 0.70), with injected drift correctly downgrading the verdict. The affect measure demonstrates the instrument; the instrument is the contribution.

Open models — is the copy valid?

Distilled the dissertation's validated GPT-4 judge into open-weight models (Qwen, Mistral) under hash-chained preregistration with mechanical verdicts: the copies matched the teacher's scores at r = 0.74 yet recovered only 0.54 validity against held-out feeling thermometers, versus the teacher's 0.70 — agreement certifies the copy, not the judge. Extended into a 29-judge ladder trained run-by-run under experimenter control (two model families, three sizes, three data doses, mid-training snapshots, a scrambled-label control) to test whether teacher-agreement is a sufficient statistic for criterion validity, under a sealed single-read design: air-gapped criterion, append-only decision log, control gates that bind the experimenter before results are seen. The judges demonstrate the method; the audit regime is the contribution.
