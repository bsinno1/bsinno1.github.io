Barea M. Sinno

Ph.D., Political Science
Department of Political Science, Rutgers University

I am a political scientist working at the intersection of political behavior and natural language processing. My research asks how ideology and political affect can be measured from text: what people actually say about policy, about their own side, and about the other side — and how those expressions move over time.

My dissertation develops two complementary measurement strategies on the American National Election Studies (ANES) instrument: an affect chapter that recovers interpersonal and partisan sentiment from open-ended likes/dislikes responses and validates it against feeling-thermometer scores, and an ideology chapter that combines expert annotation with large language models to place most-important-problem responses on a multi-dimensional ideological space. Earlier work, with collaborators at UT Austin and the University of Pittsburgh, produced the first diachronic corpus of news articles annotated for paragraph-level ideology by trained political scientists and linguists, and a dataset of U.S. Congress members' tweets annotated for interpersonal emotion.

Before graduate school I spent a decade in banking strategy in Beirut and Montreal, where I authored the bank's annual report and led a restructuring of its credit card division. I have also taught for two decades as adjunct faculty at Rutgers, Texas A&M, and Université Saint-Joseph.
